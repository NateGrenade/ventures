# Toolroom Repair Job Handoff Tracking

## Problem statement

Toolroom supervisors coordinate mold, die, fixture, and repair jobs that move among toolmakers and stations. Practitioners describe shops relying on an owner's memory, whiteboards, spreadsheets, colored paper folders, and travelers, with parts and prints going missing when progress is not captured.

## Evidence

- [type: practitioner] (2025-05) A machinist in a mold shop says eight people working on parts of three molds rely on the owner to remember job status, causing slowdowns, mistakes, missing parts, and lost prints; replies describe whiteboards, spreadsheets, work orders, travelers, and colored paper folders used to track work. https://www.reddit.com/r/Machinists/comments/1kygdeg
- [type: practitioner] (2026-05) A manufacturing practitioner seeking task management for an internal toolroom lists mold repair, press tools, dies, fixtures, rework, revisions, outside processes, and quality checks, and describes jobs hopping between people and stations with handoffs that nobody captures. https://www.reddit.com/r/manufacturing/comments/1t0fa48/task_management_for_toolrooms/
- [type: job-posting] (accessed 2026-09; posting date not stated) A Sonoco tool-and-die-maker posting requires detailed work-order documentation for maintenance and repairs, receiving tools with paperwork, and maintaining logbooks. https://sonoco.wd1.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/CorporateCareers/job/Tool-and-Die-Maker_JR-158449

## Automation hypothesis

SPECULATIVE. A shop-floor handoff tool could create one repair record per mold or die, attach the current drawing and traveler, capture each station transition with minimal input, and surface missing parts, blocked outside work, and overdue quality approval. This depends on an interface toolmakers will use during production and on linking the record to the shop's existing work-order identifier without duplicating closeout work.
