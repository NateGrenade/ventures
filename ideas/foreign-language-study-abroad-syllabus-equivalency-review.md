# Foreign-Language Study-Abroad Syllabus Equivalency Review

## Problem statement

When a student takes a language or literature course abroad that is not already
in a university's pre-approved articulation table, responsibility for deciding
whether it satisfies a home-institution requirement falls to the language
department itself — typically the department chair or a designated faculty
member — rather than to the registrar's transfer-credit system. The reviewer
receives a syllabus (sometimes in the target language), compares its content
and level by hand to home course offerings, and returns a decision on a
department-specific paper or PDF form. The registrar only updates the official
transcript after the host institution's transcript separately arrives, "which
may be months after the student's return" in at least one documented case.
This is recurring, per-student, per-course manual document comparison with no
integration between the study-abroad office's intake, the department's
subject-matter review, and the registrar's degree-audit system.

## Automation hypothesis

SPECULATIVE. A tool that let a department reviewer upload or forward a
foreign-institution syllabus (including non-English text) and get a structured
side-by-side comparison against the home catalog's course descriptions and
learning outcomes — using translation plus retrieval against the institution's
own catalog data — could compress the manual read-and-compare step and produce
a structured equivalency record the registrar could ingest directly, instead
of a decision that lives only on a department-specific PDF form until a
transcript arrives months later. This would require buy-in from the
department (the actual decision-maker, per Pace's "sole authority" language)
and an integration surface into whatever transfer-articulation module the
registrar's SIS uses (e.g., Banner, as referenced in the Wyoming posting);
neither is confirmed to be tractable from public sources, and the market here
is one department-level buyer at a time unless sold through a study-abroad or
registrar's-office platform instead.
